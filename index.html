Automation

Catching fake applicants

Rings of fake applicants reused phones, emails and resumes, and recruiters lost hours to them.

101of 7,571 applicants flagged high risk, no real hires among them

The whole story
What we built
A risk score built from signals a person can check, like a phone number, email or resume that shows up on other applications. It flags likely fakes before anyone gets on a call with them.
What changed
It flagged 101 of 7,571 applicants as high risk. None of the people who were actually hired were flagged.

Questions

Asked before signing up.

I’m not technical. How do I write a request?

The way you’d explain it to a colleague. “Customers should be able to reschedule their own bookings” is a complete request. If we need more, we ask.

What counts as one request?

One thing you could describe in a sentence or two: a new page, a report, a fix, a connection between two tools. Bigger projects get split into requests that each ship on their own, so you see progress every few days instead of waiting months.

What happens when I pause?

Billing stops and the unused days of your month wait for you. When you unpause, the queue picks up where it left off. Your software keeps running the whole time.

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit. It lives in your repository and runs in your accounts, so you can take it anywhere, with or without us.

Can you work on software we already have?

Yes. Plenty of the work above started as someone else’s code, an AI-built prototype or a pile of no-code tools. Often the first request is just getting it stable.
